Commercial Engagement Rates

For engineers who would like to be paid to audit software I give away.

Every so often a message arrives offering a comprehensive audit of my open-source repositories. The work is to be run as a formal consulting engagement, with a service level agreement, milestones and a commercial fee structure. It ends by asking for the official email address to which the proposal should be dispatched.

Thank you. To save us both time, my rates are below.

Rate card

ServiceWho does itFee
Writing the softwareMe$0.00
Giving it awayMe$0.00
Comprehensive code auditYou$0.00
Regression analysisYou$0.00
Holistic enterprise-grade diagnosticsUnclear$0.00
Drafting the service level agreementAlready done$0.00

The fee is zero in both directions. I think of it as a very balanced structure.

The service level agreement

It already exists. It is the licence at the bottom of most of my repositories, and you accepted it when you cloned one:

THE SOFTWARE IS PROVIDED “AS IS”, WITHOUT WARRANTY OF ANY KIND, EXPRESS OR IMPLIED …
— The MIT License

The service level is none. I have met it every year since the first commit.

Milestones

  1. You find a bug.
  2. You open an issue.
  3. Somebody fixes it, quite possibly you.

No invoice is raised at any milestone.

“If your team is ready”

The team is me. It is ready. It has been ready for years, and nobody pays it either.

Where to dispatch your proposal

There is no official email address. There is an issue tracker on every repository, at github.com/microprediction. Issues are free, like everything else here. Pull requests are also free, and better.

If you want to be paid

Auditing open-source code for money is a real job. Bug bounties, security programmes, grant-funded reviews and the companies that run the code in production all pay for it. The person who wrote it for nothing is the one party in the whole arrangement guaranteed not to have a budget.

Rates are reviewed annually. They have not moved.
